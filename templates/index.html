Where the numbers come from

  1. Soil values (N/P/K/pH) — you enter these from a soil test; no API can guess what is in your specific field.
  2. Location — read from your device's actual GPS via the browser, then reverse geocoded to a place name. Far more accurate than IP-based location, which in India can be off by hundreds of km.
  3. Climate values — fetched live from Open-Meteo for your coordinates.
  4. Crop model — a RandomForest classifier trained on a published 2,200-row dataset, evaluated on a held-out 20% test split.

Step 2

Soil health analysis

Enter your soil test values to get a health score, nutrient status, and fertilizer guidance. This uses the classification bands from India's Soil Health Card scheme — a transparent rule engine, not a black box.

Scoring…

How this is scored

Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium and Organic Carbon are each classified Low / Medium / High against published Soil Health Card bands. pH is checked against the 6.0-7.5 optimal range. These combine into a 0-100 score. Fertilizer amounts shown are general guidance — confirm exact dosage with a local soil-testing lab.

        Honesty about data

        What's real, and what's a labeled fallback

        Every module is tagged with where its numbers actually come from — nothing here pretends to be live when it isn't.

        Crop model — real

        Trained on a published 2,200-sample dataset. Accuracy is measured on a held-out test split, shown live in the panel above.

        Weather & location — real

        Live weather from Open-Meteo, and location from your device's GPS via the browser — not IP-based guessing.

        Market prices — real, limited coverage

        A regression model trained on real government mandi data currently covers potato, tomato and wheat. Other crops show an explicit "not covered" message rather than an invented number. Add a data.gov.in key for live prices on more crops.

        Fertilizer & calendar — real reference data

        Published package-of-practices N-P-K doses and crop-duration figures — transparent lookups and arithmetic, not ML.

        Disease check — reference, not image AI

        Symptom-matching against known diseases, not photo analysis. I looked for a real bundleable image model and found one, but its only dependency path (fastai 1.x) doesn't install on modern Python — so it's not shipped rather than shipped broken.

        Dashboard, map, knowledge base — real, with limits

        Dashboard charts are your own logged data — nothing synthetic. The map uses free Leaflet/OpenStreetMap (no key) and shows real markets, but skips "nearby weather stations" since that data doesn't exist for a model-based forecast service. The knowledge base has no video links, since I can't verify a URL is real or current without a search tool in this build.

        Krushi
        An agricultural decision-support tool. Recommendations are informational and should be combined with local agronomic advice, especially for high-value or first-time crops.
        Data sources: Open-Meteo (weather), browser GPS + reverse geocoding (location), a published crop-recommendation dataset (crop model), real Agmarknet mandi records (market model).
